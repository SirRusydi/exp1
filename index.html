Learning Objective

To study the effect of concentration, temperature, and catalyst on the reaction rate.

The reaction rate is the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time. Because both experiments measure the fixed time t required for a distinct visual endpoint to occur, the relative reaction rate is inversely proportional to time (Rate ∝ 1/t).

Part (A) Clock Reaction Equation

Na₂S₂O₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + SO₂(g) + S(s)

Insoluble yellow colloidal sulfur S(s) precipitates and gradually obscures the 'X' mark beneath the conical flask.

Part (B) Redox Reaction Equation

2MnO₄⁻(aq) + 5C₂O₄²⁻(aq) + 16H⁺(aq) → 2Mn²⁺(aq) + 10CO₂(g) + 8H₂O(l)

Deep purple MnO₄⁻ is reduced to colourless Mn²⁺; MnSO₄ supplies Mn²⁺ ions as a catalyst.

Theory

How to Structure a Concise Scientific Theory Summary (3–5 sentences):

  1. Define reaction rate: State that reaction rate is the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time.
  2. State influencing factors: Mention temperature, concentration of reactants, catalyst, particle size, and pressure.
  3. Explain measurement & mathematical relation: Explain that rate is measured by observing physical/chemical changes (such as precipitate formation or colour change) and is inversely proportional to reaction time (Rate ∝ 1/t).
  4. Include reaction equations: Briefly state the reactions studied in Part A (Na₂S₂O₃ + HCl) and Part B (MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻).
Reference: Original SK025 Lab Manual Introduction Page 36
"The reaction rate is the change in concentration of the reactants or products per unit time. The factors that influence the rate of reaction are temperature, pressure, catalyst, size of particles and concentration of reactants. The rate of a reaction can be studied by observing the change in the chemical or physical properties of species involved in the reaction. The reaction rate is inversely proportional to the time of the reaction, i.e., the faster the reaction occurs, the shorter is the time for the reaction to complete."

Grammar Rule Converting Lab Manual Instructions into Passive Past Tense

[Object / Apparatus] + [was / were] + [Past Participle (V3)]

In a scientific lab report, the procedure describes what was already done objectively. Never start sentences with imperative command verbs (Place, Pipette, Stir, Pour, Record) and never use personal pronouns (I, we, my, our, you).

Section 6 • Collision Theory, Precautions & Point to Discuss

Discussion and Exercise

6A. Scientific Discussion & Experimental Precautions

Collision Theory & Observations:

  • Concentration (Part A): Higher [Na₂S₂O₃] increases the number of S₂O₃²⁻ ions per unit volume → higher frequency of effective collisions → faster formation of yellow colloidal S(s) precipitate.
  • Temperature (Part B): Higher temperature increases average kinetic energy of molecules → more particles have KE ≥ Eₐ → faster decolourization of purple MnO₄⁻ to colourless Mn²⁺.
  • Precautions: Use the same conical flask & 'X' mark paper viewed vertically from above; allow boiling tubes to equilibrate in the water bath for 3 minutes and keep tube A submerged during mixing.

6B. Exercise (Point to Discuss — SK025 Manual Page 39)

Q1 (Function of catalyst): MnSO₄ supplies Mn²⁺ ions which provide an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy (Eₐ), increasing the fraction of particles able to react without being chemically consumed.

Q2 (Meaning of 1/t): 1/t (unit: s⁻¹) is directly proportional to the average rate of reaction because reaction rate is inversely proportional to the time taken for a fixed visual endpoint change.
